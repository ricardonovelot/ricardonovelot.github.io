June: a photo journal you scroll like a feed

A photo journal for iPhone that takes the scroll mechanics of social apps and points them at your own life. Designed and built solo in Swift and UIKit.

Your own photos, your own words, your own music. June is one scroll through all three.

Role
Design and engineering, solo
Platform
iOS, Swift and UIKit
Timeline
2025 to present
Status
TestFlight, App Store release in progress

The idea

Social apps spent a decade making exploration feel effortless. The problem was never how they worked, it was what they asked us to look at. June points the same mechanics at your own life.

Designing and building it

I designed June and then built all of it in Swift and UIKit.

Nobody wants to face a blank page

Journaling apps open on an empty screen and a blinking cursor, June never starts from nothing. It opens on your life already arranged: today, yesterday, the trip you took last month, grouped without you asking for it. Writing is one tap away from a photo you already care about, and the prompt is waiting for you: What is making you feel grateful?

Working out what counts as a trip

The feed groups your photos by itself, and the trip part took two attempts. My first version used distance from home, which turns out to be a bad signal in both directions. The gym is across the city and it is obviously not a trip. The house my family drives to every December is three hundred kilometers away and it is not a new place either.

The second version looks at how often you go back instead. Somewhere you return to on a schedule belongs to your routine whatever the distance, and somewhere you went once and stayed a while gets grouped as a trip.

Memory has a soundtrack

Four seconds of the right song and you are back: the car, that summer, who you were sitting next to. A photo takes longer to do that, and sometimes it never gets there at all.

A memory in June can carry a song, your song, the one you played until it became part of the memory. The suggestions come out of what you were actually listening to at the time rather than out of what was popular that year.

How much the app should decide for you

June makes a lot of calls on your behalf. It groups your days, sets aside the screenshots and receipts that are not really memories, brings a photo back on its anniversary, and offers a song for a memory. Finding the right level of confidence for that took a few passes.

An early build went further and applied a song by itself when you had used one near that place before. I took it out. It was doing something nobody had asked it to do, and correcting it afterwards took longer than choosing the song would have. Now the picker offers a suggestion and you tap it.

I held to the same limit elsewhere in the app. An entry has no color until you pick a feeling, because I did not want June deciding what kind of day you had from your camera roll. Telling it you are not interested in a suggested song changes what June shows you and nothing about your music library.

Speed is part of the feeling

Most apps put friction between you and what you meant to do. June responds instantly and keeps everything within reach. Organizing an afternoon of photos becomes one continuous flow instead of forty confirmation dialogs.

That idea runs through everything. Interactions happen under your thumb, and background tasks stay in the background. The app moves at the speed of your decisions, not its own.

Where it is now

June is on TestFlight, with the App Store release in progress.
